## The Prescription Is the Beginning, Not the End

Whether you are starting a weight management medication, beginning peptide therapy, or accessing medical cannabis for chronic pain, the first prescription is just the starting point. What happens next determines whether your treatment actually works.

Consider what happens in the weeks and months after starting a new treatment:

- Your body adjusts to the medication, sometimes with side effects that need to be managed
- Dosages often need to be titrated up or adjusted based on your individual response
- Your health goals may evolve as you begin to see (or not see) results
- Questions arise that you did not think to ask during your initial consultation
- Your circumstances may change — new medications, new health conditions, lifestyle shifts

Without scheduled follow-up care, patients are left to figure all of this out on their own. Some push through side effects unnecessarily. Others stop treatment prematurely because they did not realise their dose needed adjustment. And some simply lose momentum because there is nobody checking in.

## What Good Follow-Up Care Looks Like

Genuine follow-up care is not a courtesy call. It is a structured part of the treatment plan. Here is what it should include:

### Scheduled Check-In Appointments
Regular consultations with your prescriber — not just when you feel something is wrong, but as a proactive part of your care. These appointments are used to:

- Review how you are responding to treatment
- Adjust dosages based on your progress and tolerance
- Discuss any side effects and how to manage them
- Update your treatment plan as your goals evolve
- Order follow-up blood work if clinically indicated

### Accessible Support Between Appointments
You should be able to reach a real person when you have a question or concern. Not a chatbot. Not a generic inbox that takes a week to reply. A support team that knows your case and can help you promptly.

### Outcome Tracking
Your provider should be tracking your progress over time — not just relying on your memory of how things have been going. This allows for data-informed decisions about your treatment plan.

## The Evidence for Follow-Up Care

This is not just our opinion — the evidence is clear:

- Patients who receive regular follow-up care have significantly better medication adherence
- Treatment outcomes improve when dosages are adjusted based on monitored response rather than fixed protocols
- Early detection of side effects through scheduled monitoring reduces complications and improves safety
- Patient satisfaction and trust increase when they feel genuinely supported throughout their treatment

For treatments like GLP-1 medications for weight management, clinical guidelines specifically recommend regular monitoring during dose titration and ongoing treatment. The same applies to medical cannabis, where finding the right product, dose, and administration method is an iterative process that requires clinical oversight.

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

If you are considering a telehealth provider for any treatment, ask these questions:

1. **What does your follow-up care include?** Look for specific answers — scheduled appointments, not just "we are available if you need us"
2. **How do I contact you between appointments?** You should have a direct line to a support team, not just a generic contact form
3. **How often will I see my prescriber after starting treatment?** The answer should be proactive and scheduled, not reactive
4. **What happens if I experience side effects?** There should be a clear process for getting timely support
5. **Do you track my progress over time?** Your provider should be monitoring your outcomes, not just issuing repeat prescriptions
